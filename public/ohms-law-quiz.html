Before You Start

Ohm's Law connects voltage, current, and resistance. This quiz uses ideal DC sources and resistive loads unless otherwise stated. It is calculation practice; the voltage, current, and resistance quiz provides the beginner concept review.

V = I × R

Unit reminders: 1 A = 1,000 mA; 1 mA = 1,000 µA; 1 kΩ = 1,000 Ω. For resistor power, use P = V × I.

Simple pattern: Voltage pushes, resistance limits, and current changes.
Common mistake: Do not memorize the formula only. Pay attention to what changes first in the question.

Choose one answer for each question. A calculator is welcome. Convert mA to A and kΩ to Ω before substituting values; round only when a question requests it.

If a calculation question gives voltage and resistance, use I = V ÷ R. If it gives voltage and current, use R = V ÷ I. If it gives current and resistance, use V = I × R.

Your Result

Worked Answers

Compare your calculation with the formula, unit conversion, and result for each question.

Question 1 — 0.08 A: I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 150 = 0.08 A, or 80 mA.
Question 2 — 20 mA: Convert 1.2 kΩ to 1,200 Ω. I = 24 ÷ 1,200 = 0.02 A = 20 mA.
Question 3 — 10.2 V: Convert 15 mA to 0.015 A. V = I × R = 0.015 × 680 = 10.2 V.
Question 4 — 300 Ω: I = 0.030 A. R = V ÷ I = 9 ÷ 0.030 = 300 Ω.
Question 5 — 2 mA: R = 2,500 Ω. I = 5 ÷ 2,500 = 0.002 A = 2 mA.
Question 6 — 15 Ω: R = V ÷ I = 6 ÷ 0.4 = 15 Ω.
Question 7 — 9.4 V: 200 mA = 0.2 A. V = 0.2 × 47 = 9.4 V.
Question 8 — 3 V: 12 mA = 0.012 A. V = 0.012 × 250 = 3 V.
Question 9 — 10 mA: I = 3.3 ÷ 330 = 0.01 A = 10 mA.
Question 10 — 2 kΩ: 250 µA = 0.000250 A. R = 0.5 ÷ 0.000250 = 2,000 Ω = 2 kΩ.
Question 11 — 40 mA: Series resistance is 100 + 200 = 300 Ω. I = 12 ÷ 300 = 0.04 A = 40 mA. Both series resistors carry the same current.
Question 12 — 8 V: Total resistance is 500 Ω. I = 10 ÷ 500 = 0.02 A. The 400 Ω drop is 0.02 × 400 = 8 V; the other resistor drops 2 V.
Question 13 — 40 mA: Each branch carries 24 ÷ 1,200 = 20 mA. Total current is 20 + 20 = 40 mA.
Question 14 — 40 mA: Each parallel branch has the full 12 V across it. For the 300 Ω branch, I = 12 ÷ 300 = 40 mA. The total source current would be 60 mA.
Question 15 — 15 mA: R = 12 ÷ 0.020 = 600 Ω. At 9 V, I = 9 ÷ 600 = 0.015 A = 15 mA.
Question 16 — 33.3 mA: I scales as V ÷ R. New current = 50 × 2 ÷ 3 = 33.333… mA, which rounds to 33.3 mA.
Question 17 — 150 Ω: Use the present measurements: R = 12 ÷ 0.080 = 150 Ω. Its normal resistance was 120 Ω.
Question 18 — 9 V: Use the measured current: V = 0.090 × 100 = 9 V. At 120 mA the same resistance would have 12 V across it.
Question 19 — 8 V: Total resistance is 125 Ω, so I = 10 ÷ 125 = 0.08 A. The load voltage is 0.08 × 100 = 8 V. The connector drops 2 V.
Question 20 — 4 V: V = I × R = 0.016 × 250 = 4 V. For comparison, 4 mA produces 1 V and 20 mA produces 5 V.
Question 21 — 12 mA: I = 3 ÷ 250 = 0.012 A = 12 mA.
Question 22 — 1.5 kΩ: 8 mA = 0.008 A. R = 12 ÷ 0.008 = 1,500 Ω = 1.5 kΩ.
Question 23 — 0.96 W: I = 24 ÷ 600 = 0.04 A. P = V × I = 24 × 0.04 = 0.96 W. Equivalently, P = V² ÷ R.
Question 24 — 0.5 W: 50 mA = 0.05 A. P = V × I = 10 × 0.05 = 0.5 W.
Question 25 — 300 Ω: Total resistance is R = 12 ÷ 0.030 = 400 Ω. Subtract the known 100 Ω: the other resistor is 300 Ω.
