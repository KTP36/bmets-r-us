What You Will Practice

The four TEAS preparation areas

Each section rewards a slightly different way of thinking. Treating every question the same is one of the easiest ways to waste study time.

📖

Reading

Practice identifying main ideas, supporting details, purpose, tone, inference, sequence, and information found in passages or visual material.

➗

Mathematics

Build confidence with arithmetic, fractions, decimals, percentages, ratios, proportions, algebra basics, measurement, and word problems.

🧬

Science

Review anatomy and physiology, body systems, scientific reasoning, biology and chemistry fundamentals, and common cause-and-effect relationships.

✍️

English & Language

Practice grammar, punctuation, sentence structure, spelling, word meaning, and choosing language that is clear and appropriate.

Better Review

Don't treat every wrong answer the same.

Labeling why you missed a question makes practice much more useful. Four common patterns show up repeatedly.

Content gap

You did not know the underlying fact or concept. Fix this with a short focused review, then immediately answer several related questions.

Recognition gap

You knew the material but did not recognize what the question was really asking. Practice identifying keywords and question patterns.

Process error

You used the wrong calculation, skipped a step, or selected an answer before finishing the reasoning process. Slow the process down before trying to speed it up.

Reading error

You missed words such as best, first, most likely, or except. Build the habit of identifying the task before evaluating choices.

High-value habit: Keep a short missed-question list. If the same concept appears more than once, move it to your priority study list.
Mini Diagnostic

Try four TEAS-style questions before you start.

These are quick examples to get your brain into test mode. Open the answer only after choosing your response.

READING

A paragraph repeatedly explains how sleep improves concentration and memory. What is the most likely main idea?

A. Sleep is difficult to measure   B. Sleep supports learning   C. Memory is permanent   D. Concentration is unrelated to rest

Show answer

B. The repeated supporting details point to sleep supporting learning and cognitive performance.

MATH

A student correctly answers 36 of 45 practice questions. What percentage is correct?

A. 70%   B. 75%   C. 80%   D. 90%

Show answer

C. 36 ÷ 45 = 0.80, or 80%.

SCIENCE

Which organ system is primarily responsible for transporting oxygen and nutrients throughout the body?

A. Cardiovascular   B. Integumentary   C. Endocrine   D. Urinary

Show answer

A. The cardiovascular system circulates blood carrying oxygen and nutrients to tissues.

ENGLISH

Which sentence is written most clearly?

A. The student, because studying, passed.   B. The student passed because she studied consistently.   C. Studying the student passed because.   D. Because passed, the student studied.

Show answer

B. It uses clear sentence structure and expresses the relationship between studying and the result.

Interpret Your Results

What should you do after the practice test?

Use these ranges as study-direction guidance—not as a prediction of an official exam result.

Strong area

If you are consistently accurate and can explain why the answer is correct, maintain the skill with shorter review sessions.

Developing area

If you understand most concepts but make repeated mistakes, focus on patterns and complete short targeted sets before another full test.

Priority area

If questions feel unfamiliar rather than merely difficult, return to foundational learning before relying on repeated test attempts.

Study Smarter

A simple one-week improvement cycle

You do not need to study every topic every day. Use your baseline to concentrate effort where it has the greatest value.

Days 1–2: Diagnose

Take mixed practice and review every miss. Identify the one or two sections responsible for most errors.

Days 3–5: Target

Use focused practice, anatomy tools, terminology, math review, and other MedSkillBuilder resources for your weakest areas.

Days 6–7: Retest

Return to mixed practice. Compare not only the score but also how quickly you recognize what each question is asking.

FAQ

Free TEAS practice test questions

Is the MedSkillBuilder TEAS practice test free?

Yes. MedSkillBuilder provides free TEAS-style practice and related learning tools without requiring a paid subscription.

What subjects should I practice?

Build preparation across reading, mathematics, science, and English and language usage. Your practice results should determine which area receives the most extra study time.

Should I keep taking full practice tests every day?

Not necessarily. A better approach is mixed practice, missed-question review, targeted study, and then another mixed practice session to measure improvement.

What if science is my weakest section?

Return to foundational anatomy, physiology, terminology, and science relationships. Practice questions are more useful after the concepts feel familiar enough to recognize.

What if math is my weakest section?

Identify the specific process causing errors—fractions, percentages, ratios, algebra, measurement, or word-problem translation—and practice that process separately before returning to mixed questions.
