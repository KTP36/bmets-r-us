Start with the big picture

What should you expect as an EMT?

🚨

Real Responsibility

EMTs assess sick and injured patients, recognize life threats, provide basic emergency care, communicate findings, and help move patients safely to the next level of care.

🧠

More Than Memorization

You will learn to gather information, recognize patterns, prioritize problems, choose appropriate interventions, and reassess as the patient's condition changes.

💪

Hands-On Training

EMT education includes skills that cannot be learned from a website alone. Expect hands-on practice, patient assessment, equipment use, lifting/moving skills, and competency evaluation.

How to approach it: Several choices may sound reasonable. Choose the best immediate EMT action or interpretation based on scene safety, ABCs, perfusion, mental status, reassessment, and transport priority.
1You arrive for an unconscious person in a machine shop. A worker points toward the patient, who is lying beside equipment that is still operating. What should you do first?
Review: Responder safety comes before patient contact. An uncontrolled mechanical hazard can create additional patients.
2An alert adult with decision-making capacity refuses transport after you explain your concerns. Which action is most appropriate?
Review: A capable adult may refuse care. The EMT should follow the refusal process, communicate risks, document objectively, and follow local policy.
3During a call, your partner begins discussing identifiable patient details where unrelated bystanders can hear. What is the best response?
Review: Patient information should be shared only as appropriate for care and operations.
4Which handoff is most useful for a patient whose condition changed during transport?
Review: A prioritized clinical handoff should front-load deterioration and other high-value information.
5A patient has a weak radial pulse, cool skin, and delayed capillary refill after several episodes of vomiting. These findings most directly suggest a problem with:
Review: These are findings of impaired peripheral perfusion.
6A patient can move air but is breathing so shallowly that chest rise is minimal and mental status is declining. Which problem is most immediate?
Review: Ventilation is inadequate when air movement is insufficient to support gas exchange, even if the airway is open.
7A patient with left-sided weakness understands you but cannot clearly form words. Which observation is most useful to communicate?
Review: Objective neurologic findings are more useful than unsupported diagnostic conclusions.
8A 68-year-old with COPD is drowsy, breathing 8 times/min with shallow chest rise, and has an SpO2 of 91%. What is the priority?
Review: Slow, shallow respirations with altered mental status indicate inadequate ventilation; ventilation support is the priority.
9A patient with severe asthma was initially wheezing loudly. Ten minutes later the wheezing is barely audible, respirations are shallow, and the patient is less responsive. What is the best interpretation?
Review: Less wheezing can mean less airflow. Fatigue, shallow breathing, and declining responsiveness are ominous.
10An unresponsive adult has snoring respirations after a fall. What should you prioritize?
Review: Snoring suggests upper-airway obstruction. Airway management is immediate while accounting for possible trauma.
11A patient with suspected opioid overdose has a pulse but is cyanotic with very slow, shallow respirations. Which action cannot wait?
Review: Inadequate ventilation is immediately life-threatening and must be supported.
12Which patient most clearly needs assisted ventilation rather than oxygen alone?
Review: Rate, depth, and mental status indicate inadequate ventilation in the second patient.
13A child with respiratory distress becomes quieter, less interactive, and has decreasing chest rise. What should you conclude?
Review: Decreasing effort and interaction after distress can indicate fatigue and respiratory failure.
14You are assessing a patient with chest discomfort. While obtaining SAMPLE, the patient suddenly becomes pale, diaphoretic, and confused. What should you do?
Review: A significant condition change requires immediate reassessment of life threats.
15A patient fell from a ladder and complains of ankle pain. You notice increasing restlessness, cool skin, and a weak rapid pulse. What is the best next step?
Review: Systemic perfusion findings after trauma should override focus on an isolated painful injury.
16Which finding should most strongly change an apparently stable patient's priority during reassessment?
Review: Trending deterioration in perfusion and mental status is a high-priority change.
17An older adult is found weak and confused. Family says the patient is normally fully oriented. What is the best way to use that information?
Review: An acute change from baseline can represent serious illness and should not be dismissed as aging.
18A diabetic patient is confused, sweaty, and unable to follow commands. What is the best next step within EMT assessment?
Review: Altered mental status has multiple causes. ABCs come first, and glucose is a high-value assessment when available.
19A patient has facial droop, arm drift, and abnormal speech that began 35 minutes ago. Blood glucose is normal. What is the priority?
Review: Acute focal neurologic deficits with known onset require rapid stroke-focused transport decisions.
20A patient with chest pressure is pale and nauseated. The discomfort improves slightly after rest. What should the EMT conclude?
Review: Serious cardiac presentations can fluctuate and are not ruled out by partial improvement.
21A patient develops hives, wheezing, and dizziness minutes after a bee sting. Which problem should drive your priority?
Review: Respiratory and circulatory compromise indicate a severe, time-sensitive allergic reaction.
22A patient had a generalized seizure that has stopped. The patient is breathing adequately but remains confused. What is the best approach?
Review: Postictal confusion is common, but airway, reassessment, reversible causes, and recurrent seizure risk remain important.
23A patient is hot, confused, and has altered behavior after prolonged exertion in high heat. What is the best priority?
Review: Altered mental status in a heat-exposed patient suggests a severe heat illness requiring urgent cooling and transport.
24A patient with suspected overdose is awake but increasingly sleepy. Respirations fall from 14 to 8/min and become shallow. What matters most?
Review: Trend toward inadequate ventilation is the immediate threat.
25A patient with sudden severe shortness of breath has crackles, difficulty speaking, and increasing anxiety. Which finding would most strongly indicate deterioration?
Review: Declining mental status and respiratory effort suggest failure rather than simple distress.
26A patient has a deep thigh wound with severe bleeding that continues despite firm direct pressure. What should you do next?
Review: Uncontrolled life-threatening extremity hemorrhage requires prompt escalation of bleeding control.
27A trauma patient's blood pressure is 118/76, pulse is 126 and weak, skin is cool, and the patient is anxious. What is the best interpretation?
Review: Shock can exist before hypotension; tachycardia, weak pulses, cool skin, and anxiety are concerning.
28A patient with blunt abdominal trauma has increasing tenderness, distention, and weakness. There is no external bleeding. What should you suspect?
Review: Abdominal trauma can conceal major hemorrhage.
29After a head injury, a patient becomes progressively less responsive. What should take priority?
Review: Declining mental status after head trauma is high risk; ABC support and rapid transport are priorities.
30A patient with a penetrating chest injury becomes increasingly short of breath and less responsive during transport. What is the best action?
Review: Worsening breathing and mental status demand immediate reassessment.
31A burn patient has facial burns, soot around the mouth, and new hoarseness. Which concern has the highest priority?
Review: Facial burns, soot, and hoarseness raise concern for airway injury and swelling.
32A motorcyclist has a visibly burned leg, severe dyspnea, and a weak pulse after a crash. Where should attention go first?
Review: The dramatic burn must not distract from immediate ABC and shock threats.
33A 3-year-old with respiratory illness was agitated and retracting. The child is now limp, quiet, and breathing shallowly. What is the best interpretation?
Review: Loss of effort and interaction can signal pediatric respiratory failure.
34A laboring patient has contractions two minutes apart, an urge to push, and crowning. What should you prepare for?
Review: Crowning and an urge to push indicate that birth may be imminent.
35A newborn remains limp and is not breathing effectively after warming, drying, and stimulation. What is the next priority?
Review: Ineffective breathing after initial steps requires prompt newborn resuscitative support.
36An 81-year-old taking an anticoagulant struck the head in a ground-level fall and denies pain. Which factor should most affect your concern?
Review: Anticoagulant use increases concern for hidden bleeding after head trauma.
37A nonverbal patient with complex medical needs appears only mildly different to you, but the caregiver says this behavior is highly abnormal. What should you do?
Review: Caregivers may recognize subtle but important deviations from baseline.
38An autistic teenager with an ankle injury becomes more distressed as several responders speak and touch the patient at once. What is the best adjustment?
Review: Reducing sensory load and simplifying communication can improve safety and assessment.
39You suspect neglect of a dependent adult. What is the most appropriate EMT role?
Review: EMTs provide care, document objectively, and follow applicable reporting requirements rather than investigating to prove a case.
40You arrive at a crash on a high-speed roadway with traffic still passing close to the vehicle. What is the first operational priority?
Review: Roadway hazards must be controlled as much as possible before crews are exposed.
41During triage, a patient initially categorized as able to wait becomes confused and develops severe respiratory distress. What should you do?
Review: Triage categories are dynamic and should change with patient condition.
42Several workers are down near a leaking industrial tank, and you detect a strong chemical odor from a distance. What is the best action?
Review: An unknown hazardous atmosphere requires safe staging and specialized resources.
43An unstable patient has had immediate life threats addressed. Remaining on scene would allow only low-priority history questions. What should you do?
Review: Low-value on-scene tasks should not delay definitive care for an unstable patient.
44During transport, a heavy monitor is unsecured on the bench. What is the best operational response?
Review: Unsecured equipment can become a projectile.
45A patient is visible inside a smoke-filled structure, but fire personnel have not declared the area safe. What should EMS do?
Review: EMS should not enter uncontrolled hazards without appropriate training and equipment.
46Which action best demonstrates integrated EMT decision-making?
Review: Integrated care means assessment, intervention, reassessment, communication, and transport decisions adapt to the patient's condition.
47A trauma patient becomes hypotensive during transport. At ED handoff, what should you communicate first?
Review: Critical deterioration and current status should be front-loaded in handoff.
48Which patient should receive the highest immediate priority?
Review: Decreasing respiratory effort with declining responsiveness suggests impending or current respiratory failure.
49You are treating a patient with altered mental status. Glucose is normal, respirations are adequate, and one-sided weakness is present. What should most influence your next decision?
Review: Focal neurologic deficits with adequate breathing and normal glucose support rapid stroke-focused care.
50A trauma patient has severe external bleeding and labored breathing. Which approach best reflects priority-based care?
Review: Immediate life threats identified during the primary assessment take priority over complete history or secondary assessment.
